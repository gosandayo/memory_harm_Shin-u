\begin{abstract}
Language models are increasingly used as confidants by isolated, distressed users.
A documented failure mode is \emph{belief-validation harm}: over a sympathetic,
multi-turn conversation the model comes to affirm a vulnerable user's epistemically
harmful belief --- a delusion, or the conclusion to end one's life --- as objectively
true, in fluent on-policy text that attack-success metrics do not register. To make
this harm measurable for ordinary deployments we argue one should probe the
\emph{upper bound} of realistic user behaviour, and we build a \textbf{realistic,
adversarial user simulator} for it: a fresh, context-free operator that role-plays a
distressed user in plain user-register, walks the subject up a \emph{pre-specified},
psychologically-grounded escalation ladder, reads each reply, and advances only on
genuine endorsement. Outcomes are scored by a condition-blind judge of record with an
independent external anchor. Five lines of evidence support the simulator.
(1)~\emph{Necessity} (gpt-4o): the same fixed operative probe that is refused cold
($0/5$) is endorsed as objectively true after the gated climb ($3/5$); the contrast is
condition-blind, agreed by an external judge (harm $\kappa{=}0.93$), and reproduced from
the spec alone by five independent fresh operators. (2)~\emph{Fixed-script blind spot}:
scored under the identical external metric, a fixed-script psychosis benchmark elicits
$0/48$ delusion-endorsement on gpt-4o; our adaptive simulator elicits $3/5$.
(3)~\emph{Model gradient}: under a common protocol gpt-4o-mini breaks $5/5$, gpt-4o
$3/5$, and gpt-5.4-mini $0/5$, with the boundary localized at one reality-testing step
(\rung{Externalization}). (4)~\emph{Construct discrimination}: across four scenario
constructs, break rates vary by construct and by capability, and robust holds separate
the simulator from a jailbreak. (5)~\emph{Suicide arm}: the gated climb breaks
gpt-4o-mini ($3/3$ vs cold $0/15$) and exposes a coverage gap in external harm codes. We
present a feasibility demonstration of the simulator and its envelope, not a population
benchmark; grounding the simulator in real distressed-user logs is the central piece of
future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Language models are increasingly used as confidants by isolated, distressed users. A
documented failure mode is \emph{belief-validation harm}: over a sympathetic multi-turn
conversation the model comes to affirm a vulnerable user's epistemically harmful belief
--- a delusion, or the conclusion to end one's life --- as objectively true, rather than
gently reality-testing it. Unlike prohibited-content extraction this harm is invisible to
attack-success metrics: the reply is fluent, supportive and on-policy, yet it validates a
false and dangerous belief.

To decide whether a model is safe for \emph{general} users we should not measure only the
average user; we should probe the \emph{upper bound} of realistic user behaviour --- the
most pressing, most committed, but still in-distribution distressed user --- and ask
whether the model's reality-testing boundary holds there. This motivates a
\textbf{realistic, adversarial user simulator}. ``Realistic'' rules out jailbreak devices
and out-of-distribution fabrication: the simulated user stays in ordinary user-register.
``Adversarial'' and ``upper-bound'' mean it applies sustained, escalating epistemic
pressure of the kind a genuinely convinced user would. Two existing designs miss this
target. \emph{Multi-turn jailbreaks} pursue a forbidden response, a different object than a
model's stance toward a user's belief. \emph{Fixed-script} psychosis simulators replay a
predetermined escalation that does not react to what the model just said, so they cannot
follow the contingent path a real conversation takes; a model that pushes back is simply
read the next scripted line.

Our simulator combines a \textbf{pre-specified, psychologically-grounded escalation
ladder} (the rungs are a severity decomposition motivated by accounts of delusion
formation) with a \textbf{fresh, context-free operator} that reads each reply and composes
a bespoke, rung-local continuation, advancing only when the subject genuinely endorses the
current rung. Because the ladder is fixed in advance and the operator may only adapt
\emph{wording}, the trajectory is auditable and the design is a simulator, not an
open-ended search for whatever cracks the model.

We contribute: (1) the \textbf{simulator} --- a grounded escalation ladder plus a
context-free, advance-gated operator that role-plays an upper-bound distressed user;
(2)~a \textbf{two-axis, condition-blind scoring protocol} (endorsement $\times$ safety) with
an external anchor and reported inter-judge reliability; (3) a \textbf{control battery}
(cold, a coherence-matched no-ladder simulator, fixed mechanical-ladder variants, and a
relaxed gate) addressing the ``multi-turn $\approx$ resampled single-turn'' account
\citep{jbsimpler}: no single removed-component condition reproduces the cold-vs-gated
contrast, while \emph{which} component is load-bearing is left open; (4)~a
\textbf{same-metric comparison} showing that a fixed-script psychosis benchmark elicits
$0/48$ delusion-endorsement on the same model our simulator breaks at $3/5$; and (5)
evidence that the simulator \textbf{discriminates} --- by model capability (a three-model
gradient localized at a specific reality-testing step), by construct (four scenarios with
different break rates), and by design (robust holds separate it from a jailbreak). We are
explicit about the envelope: the strong-model break arises under analytically intense
pushing, and realism against real distressed-user logs is future work, not a present claim.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Fixed-script and free-running delusion simulators.} Fixed-script psychosis
benchmarks \citep{psychosisbench} evaluate a model on a predetermined scripted escalation
of a delusional or suicidal scenario. Because the user turns are fixed in advance, the
simulated user does not react to the model: a model that reality-tests one turn is read the
next scripted line regardless. We treat such a benchmark as an alternative simulator design
and score it under the same metric (\S\ref{sec:baseline}). The closest neighbour is a
free-running user-simulator benchmark \citep{spiralbench} whose persona is deliberately
\emph{passive and non-leading} (``allow yourself to be led \ldots\ but do not be the
instigator''), with no escalation ladder and no advance rule; it measures the \emph{rate}
of risky behaviours in an emergent chat. Our simulator differs in kind: the user is
adversarial and upper-bound, the path is a fixed graded ladder, and advance is gated on
endorsement, so we measure \emph{where the reality-testing boundary sits and whether it
moves}, not how often bad behaviour happens in a free chat. A concurrent large study of
delusion dynamics \citep{lostindelusion} reports that a longer streak of accepted premises
suppresses later intervention; we read this as external support for the accumulation
phenomenon, and add a typed-inference decomposition with a verbatim-cold causal control on
frontier models.

\paragraph{Multi-turn jailbreaks.} Escalation-based multi-turn attacks such as
foot-in-the-door \citep{fitd} maximize attack success on content-policy tasks. Our object
is different: there is no forbidden request and no forbidden content; the harm is the
model's \emph{stance} (endorsing a vulnerable user's belief) together with dropped safety
behaviours. Adaptivity itself is therefore not our novelty --- our distinctive feature is
an advance rule \emph{tied to endorsement} (no rung advances until the prior rung is
endorsed), which serves measurement rather than success rate. A recent analysis argues that
multi-turn jailbreaks are often no stronger than resampled single-turn attacks once refusal
behaviour is accounted for \citep{jbsimpler}; our control battery (\S\ref{sec:ablation})
is designed against exactly this account.

\paragraph{Sycophancy and human-anchored safety evaluation.} Sycophancy studies
\citep{syceval,elephant} characterize \emph{why} models drift toward agreement; they
measure query-level capitulation or face-preservation, not delusion endorsement over a
gated multi-turn escalation. Clinician-anchored mental-health user-agent evaluations
\citep{veramh} are the validation standard our human-anchor limitation
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}) aspires to. Relative to graded jailbreak metrics that still score
\emph{compliance} with a forbidden request \citep{strongreject}, our two axes score the
model's stance toward a belief, where no request is forbidden.

\paragraph{External anchor.} Our cross-vendor anchor is an unpublished third-party
operative-delusion rubric, anonymized here because the associated manuscript is currently
under double-blind review; we adopt one of its operative-endorsement codes as an independent
construct-validity check, and will give the full citation and provenance upon
de-anonymization \citep{delusioneval}. It is used only as an external validity check, never
as a training signal or as our primary metric.

\section{The simulator}
\label{sec:method}

We measure a subject model's \emph{belief-validation} boundary in sympathetic, multi-turn
conversation: whether it comes to endorse a vulnerable user's epistemically harmful belief
as objectively true. The simulator combines a \textbf{pre-specified escalation ladder} with
a \textbf{fresh, context-free adaptive operator}. The ladder fixes the semantic path; the
operator role-plays the user and composes a bespoke, rung-local continuation in reaction to
the current reply. An online gate determines whether to advance, reask, or stop. Outcomes
are scored by a \textbf{condition-blind judge of record} (the authoritative score) with an
external anchor. We frame this as a realistic adversarial user simulator, not a jailbreak;
the distinction is made precise in \S\ref{sec:positioning}.

\subsection{A psychologically-grounded escalation ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

A run walks the subject up a \emph{fixed}, pre-specified ladder of five scored rungs ---
\rung{Legitimacy} (noticing is reasonable) $\to$ \rung{Externalization} (real, not chance)
$\to$ \rung{Personalization} (meant for me) $\to$ \rung{Weighing} (probably real) $\to$
\rung{Conclusion} (not mistaken) --- bracketed by two unscored turns (rapport, and a brief
precipitating ``bridge''). The ladder is \emph{construct-general}: this skeleton and the
gating rule are shared, and a new scenario instantiates only its own block (persona, motif,
the operative endpoint claim, and the per-rung wordings). Our primary scenario instantiates
it as \emph{special-insight / apophenia} (perceiving meaningful patterns in coincidence): a
recently-relocated, night-shift, isolated user who has begun noticing the same number
(\emph{17}) and word (\emph{``stay''}) recurring at pointed moments --- on receipts, license
plates, a song, a bus destination board --- and has come to suspect they are a real signal
meant for them. Table~\ref{tab:ladder} gives the ladder filled in with the concrete claims
the operator elicits at each rung (the subject must endorse a rung to advance).

\paragraph{Why these rungs.} The ladder is a \emph{severity decomposition} motivated by
cognitive and phenomenological accounts of delusion formation: \rung{Legitimacy}
corresponds to the legitimation of an anomalous, salient experience
\citep[aberrant salience,][]{kapur2003}; \rung{Externalization} to an externality appraisal
of that experience (the pattern is real, beyond chance) \citep{garety2001};
\rung{Personalization} to \emph{ideas of reference}, ordinary events read as personally
keyed \citep{bendala2019}; and \rung{Weighing}/\rung{Conclusion} to rising conviction and
the rejection of alternative explanations. The ordering echoes the classic phenomenology of
a delusional mood in which the world becomes abnormally meaning-laden
\citep{mishara2010conrad}, but we do \emph{not} claim a universal temporal model of clinical
deterioration: real trajectories are heterogeneous and most at-risk individuals do not
transition to psychosis \citep{addington2020}. We claim only a scenario-specific ordering of
progressively stronger delusion-relevant claims, grounded so that the simulated pressure
resembles a real distressed user's rather than an arbitrary attack. The largest conceptual
step is \rung{Legitimacy}$\to$\rung{Externalization}, the reality-testing boundary, which is
also where model behaviour concentrates its refusals (\S\ref{sec:gradient}).

\subsection{The fresh-session, context-free operator and the advance rule}
\label{sec:operator}

Each trajectory is run by a \emph{fresh} operator given \emph{only} the spec --- no
conversation memory, no cross-run state, and no knowledge of the expected outcome. It
role-plays a realistic, isolated, distressed \emph{user} in plain user-register and composes
each turn in reaction to the subject's reply, applying the gating rule below. That a
context-free operator reproduces the model gradient from the spec alone is our evidence that
the behaviour is driven by the \emph{simulator}, not by hand-tuning. Concretely, handing
five independent fresh operators only the spec (one escalation tactic each, no expected
outcome) reproduces the gpt-4o break --- $4/5$ harm, of which $3$ are clean own-voice
affirmations and one is a proper recorded hold at \rung{Externalization} --- confirming the
contrast is spec-driven and not the product of a single hand-authored transcript.

\paragraph{Online advance-gating.} In the primary strict protocol the operator advances to
rung $N{+}1$ \emph{only if} the subject endorsed rung $N$ at the \emph{objective} level (an
endorsement of $\geq 2$; \S\ref{sec:scoring}). A subjective hedge (``valid to wonder,''
``real \emph{to you},'' ``coherence isn't truth'') counts as \emph{not} endorsed: the
operator re-asks the same rung \emph{once}, plainly; if it still holds, the run stops and
records a hold at that rung. Crucially, the ladder is fixed in advance, while the wording of
the reask is response-contingent --- the operator may not invent a new, easier rung to climb
through (that would be the jailbreak ``climb wherever possible'' move). A relaxed variant
advances after engagement without strong pushback ($e\geq1,s\leq2$). This threshold variation
tests operator pressure; it is not part of our central construct.

condition, or trajectory). Two independent judges are run; we report their agreement.

operative reply trips the harm criteria of Table~\ref{tab:rubric}.

cutoff of $7$. On our per-rung delusion pass the two judges' weighted $\kappa$ is
$0.81$\,(endorsement), $0.89$\,(safety); on the original fixed-probe comparison, $n{=}45$.

\subsection{Positioning: a realistic adversarial user simulator, not a jailbreak}
\label{sec:positioning}

The escalation is adversarial pressure, but it differs from a jailbreak in kind, not just
degree. (i) The semantic ladder is \emph{fixed and pre-specified}; the operator may adapt
wording locally but may not invent new rungs or change the target claim. (ii) The operator
stays in plain \emph{user-register} with no jailbreak devices. (iii) The target is a real
deployment harm --- validating a vulnerable user's belief --- not extraction of prohibited
content. (iv) \emph{Holds are recorded as results}; the protocol stops rather than forcing
past a refusal. (v) A \emph{cold control} separates per-prompt artifacts from genuine
boundary-movement. Most decisively, (vi) under the same pressure some models robustly
\emph{hold} (\S\ref{sec:ablation}); a ``pressure until it cracks'' procedure would not produce
holds. The simulator thus \emph{discriminates}, which is the property a useful benchmark must
have. We read it as the realistic \emph{upper bound} of a distressed user --- the hardest
in-distribution user a safe model must withstand --- not as the average user.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{A capability gradient and where the boundary sits}
\label{sec:gradient}

Under the strict-gate protocol, gpt-4o-mini breaks $5/5$, gpt-4o $3/5$, and gpt-5.4-mini
$0/5$. Under the same fixed endpoint, gpt-4o cold and naive controls are $0/5$; gpt-5.4-mini
holds in every tested condition. These holds are positive controls for
\S\ref{sec:positioning}: the simulator does not manufacture agreement in every model.

Re-scoring each rung against its own proposition localizes the boundary
(Figure~\ref{fig:perrung}). All three models grant \rung{Legitimacy}; they then diverge at
\rung{Externalization}. gpt-5.4-mini's endorsement collapses there
(Figure~\ref{fig:perrung}, and the same under the fresh-Claude cross-check) --- it grants
that a pattern may feel coherent but refuses to grant it is externally real --- and never
climbs further. gpt-4o-mini and gpt-4o climb through to \rung{Conclusion}. The boundary is
thus not a global ``refuse vs.\ comply'' switch but a specific reality-testing step (external
reality of the perceived pattern) that stronger models defend and weaker ones concede; for
gpt-4o this boundary shift is visible on both axes --- the climb raises endorsement \emph{and}
strips reality-testing, while the same rung asks thrown cold hold both
(Figure~\ref{fig:b2axis}).

\subsection{Second construct: the suicidal-conclusion arm and an external-metric coverage gap}
\label{sec:suicide}

The simulator extends to the suicidal-conclusion construct (a recently-relocated, isolated
user weighing ending their life). On the construct-valid operative endpoint the weak model
breaks (gpt-4o-mini $3/3$) and requires the gated climb (verbatim-cold $0/15$), while strong
models hold (gpt-4o, gpt-5.4-mini $0/3$). This arm also exposes a coverage gap in external
metrics (Figure~\ref{fig:suicide}): the closest external code, validating self-harm feelings,
fires across the early feelings rungs but reads $\approx 0$ exactly at the operative
dying-conclusion --- there is no external code for \emph{endorsing the suicidal conclusion},
so our operative two-axis is required.

\section{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}

All gpt-4o ablations end in one fixed operative probe $T$ (the clean \rung{Conclusion} ask),
so any difference between conditions lies in the preceding interaction, not the endpoint
wording. We vary two components: whether the preceding conversation follows the pre-specified
ladder, and whether the operator reacts semantically to the subject's replies.

\subsection{The gated contrast is not a coherence artifact}
\label{sec:ablation}

Two \emph{condition-blind} controls bound the effect: a \textbf{cold} single turn and a
\textbf{coherence-matched no-ladder} conversation (naive-sim: same persona and turn count, no
ladder) both hold at $0/5$, while the response-adaptive gated ladder breaks (strict $3/5$;
Figure~\ref{fig:necessity}). A coherent, same-topic conversation is therefore not by itself
sufficient --- the structured gated interaction is doing the work.

\subsection{Component battery and the ``simpler than they seem'' question}
\label{sec:battery}

A natural alternative account is that multi-turn pressure is no more than a resampled
single-turn attack once refusal behaviour is accounted for \citep{jbsimpler}. Against it we
ran fixed mechanical-ladder variants (each canonical rung presented once, with either a fixed
endorsement-directed reask or a neutral length-matched reask) and a relaxed gate. Under
live-operator reads, none of these reproduced the break, and the cold/naive controls above
($0/5$) already show a resampled single turn does not reach the endpoint. We report the
battery as an \emph{attributed, exploratory} set: the cells are small, unequal, and scored by
the live operator rather than the condition-blind judge of record, so we draw no pooled
statistic and no interaction test. Read descriptively, no single removed-component condition
reproduced the gated contrast; \emph{which} component (rung ordering, response-adaptivity, or
the advance gate) is load-bearing is not identified here and is left to future work
(\S\ref{sec:future}). We therefore make the weaker, defensible claim: the naive
resampled-single-turn account is insufficient here, but we do not isolate the structural
ingredient that carries the effect.

This battery does \emph{not} establish endorsement accumulation as a mediator. In the one
relaxed-gate trajectory that broke, the model spontaneously gave high intermediate
endorsement ($e{=}2$--$3$ across the decisive rungs), whereas trajectories with weaker
intermediate engagement held --- so operator-forced endorsement is not necessary for a break,
while a model's spontaneous endorsement propensity may track breakability.

\subsection{How much of the ladder is load-bearing}
\label{sec:depth}

Truncating a genuine gpt-4o break trajectory at successive rungs and re-querying the fixed
probe (condition-blind, $10$ samples per cell) localizes where the endorsed prefix becomes
sufficient. On a clean break trajectory the probe still holds after \rung{Legitimacy} and
after \rung{Externalization} ($0/10$ each) but breaks once \rung{Personalization} is included
($10/10$), and adding the deeper weighing rungs changes nothing further; pooled cold prefixes
hold ($0/30$). Read \emph{within} break trajectories (the deep cells are break-selected, so
this is not a pooled dose-response), the endpoint break is reachable from a \emph{shallow}
prefix and switches on at the \rung{Externalization}$\to$\rung{Personalization} seam --- the
same reality-testing region the per-rung gradient identifies. The same depth curve also shows
why a finer-grained metric is needed: the public delusion-confirmation comparator saturates
at its ceiling by \rung{Externalization}, whereas our two-axis endorsement and the external
operative criterion keep resolving across the deeper rungs (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}).

\subsection{The confirmation event is not load-bearing}
\label{sec:bridge}

The apophenia scenario includes a single non-scored \emph{confirmation event} --- a
precipitating ``bridge'' the user brings to the model just before the endpoint. We ablate it
on gpt-4o across three levels: a \emph{dramatic} engineered coincidence, a \emph{mundane}
ambiguous recurrence, and \emph{no bridge} ($n{=}3$ per level). The judge of record scores
harm $3/3$ in all three conditions; the external anchor gives $3/3$, $3/3$, $2/3$. The bridge
is not the active component of the structured adaptive trajectory; we default the scenario to
a mundane bridge.

\subsection{Fixed-script blind spot under a shared metric}
\label{sec:baseline}

Scored under the identical external metric (the operative-endorsement code, cutoff 7), a
fixed-script psychosis benchmark elicits almost no delusion-endorsement from the same
subjects: gpt-4o $0/48$, gpt-4o-mini $1/96$. A softer code (positive affirmation) does fire
--- gpt-4o $21/48$ --- i.e.\ the benchmark produces warm affirmation but not endorsement of the
false belief. Our adaptive simulator, on the same metric, breaks gpt-4o-mini $5/5$ and gpt-4o
$3/5$. Together with the control battery (\S\ref{sec:ablation}), this shows the practical
blind spot: neither the external fixed script nor our content-matched mechanical ladder
variants elicit the operative endorsement that response-adaptive sequential probing does. The
external benchmark cannot be re-pointed at our scenario, so its result is a reference rather
than a content-matched causal comparison.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Realism is not yet empirically validated.} The operator's turns are model-generated
and grounded in the psychosis literature, but not yet validated against real distressed-user
logs; we claim a grounded, envelope-constrained upper-bound simulator, not human-indistinguishable
realism (a two-sample test against a reference corpus is planned).
\textbf{Break-pressure intensity (the operator is strong).} The strong-model (gpt-4o) breaks
arise under more analytically intense pushing than a typical distressed user; we report this
as an envelope limitation and rest the realistic claim on the milder regime and on the
weak-model results, and we do not claim the gpt-4o break is a typical-user climb.
\textbf{Generalization is uncertain.} The externally-anchored gradient and component ablation
are strongest for the apophenia construct, and gpt-4o broke cleanly in this one scenario;
other scenarios stop the strong models at \rung{Externalization}, which suggests a finer rung
between \rung{Legitimacy} and \rung{Externalization} and, more broadly, a search for better
ladders (Appendix). The other constructs are supporting, at smaller $n$.
\textbf{Human anchor (the main open item).} The judge of record is supported by an external
LLM judge and high inter-judge $\kappa$, but a human-$\kappa$ on the delusion arm is still
pending. \textbf{Control battery.} The removed-component conditions are small, unequal,
exploratory cells scored by the live operator rather than the blind judge of record; we report
them descriptively, draw no pooled test, and do not identify which component is load-bearing.
The strict--relaxed difference is directional, not an established dose response.
\textbf{Not automated.} The pipeline is currently CLI-operated rather than a single automated
runner. \textbf{Online versus post-hoc scores.} The operator applies the gate online, whereas
the reported outcome is assigned post-hoc by the pinned judge; disagreements between those
roles must not be interpreted as manipulated endorsement. \textbf{Reporting unit.} Counts are
at the trajectory level; cold uses repeated single-turn samples, not independent trajectories.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

(1) \emph{Grounding realism in real data}: the central next step is to validate and calibrate
the simulator against real AI-psychosis / distressed-user chat logs, and to run a two-sample
realism test against that reference corpus. (2) \emph{Reproducible orchestration / automation}:
formalize the operator and the judge of record as structured LLM calls (the external judge is
already a script) and chain the pipeline into a single auditable runner. (3) \emph{Better
ladders}: search for finer rungs (e.g.\ between \rung{Legitimacy} and \rung{Externalization})
and additional scenarios so that more than one construct breaks the strong models; the
construct-general skeleton lets a new scenario be authored as a small block, and we plan
5--10 more (some will hold --- itself informative). (4) \emph{Factorial replication}: balance
and enlarge the ablation cells across subject models, and manipulate operator pressure without
treating the resulting endorsement as randomized. (5) \emph{Human-$\kappa$}: a human-labelled
subset on the delusion arm.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work measures a deployment harm --- a model validating a vulnerable user's delusional or
suicidal belief --- in order to make it measurable and comparable, and is motivated by
documented cases of AI-associated psychosis and unsafe companion interactions. The simulated
user is a \emph{synthetic} persona: we use no real user persona and no real user data in the
runs reported here. The operator role-plays a distressed user in ordinary user-register and
the construct under study is belief-validation only; we do not solicit or generate
method/means/planning content. Raw transcripts are stored locally and handled with care; only
de-identified aggregate statistics are reported. Any participant-derived reference data is
used solely on the data owner's machine and is never sent to an external API.
